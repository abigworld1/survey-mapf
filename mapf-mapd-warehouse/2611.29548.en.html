In short: The TAKT framework enables real-time collision-free trajectory planning for large robot fleets by converting discrete lattice plans into dynamically feasible continuous trajectories via closed-form convolutions.

What is this paper about?

Coordinating large robot fleets, such as those in automated warehouses or quadrotor swarms, requires trajectories that satisfy both collision avoidance and dynamic constraints (speed and acceleration limits). Existing kinodynamic multi-agent planners often struggle with scalability because they either incorporate dynamic constraints directly into the search space or perform expensive trajectory optimization after the initial path is found. This limitation restricts the number of robots that can be managed and the speed at which the fleet can respond to environmental changes.

What is new compared with prior work?

This paper introduces TAKT (Tickwise Actions to Kinodynamic Trajectories), a framework that decouples discrete coordination from continuous trajectory generation. A key novelty is the analytical bounding of clearance loss during trajectory smoothing, which allows the planner to reserve a safety margin directly on a discrete lattice. Furthermore, the authors introduce a 'diamond formulation' for discrete coordination, which enables one-tick sideways yielding, making it compatible with efficient MAPF solvers like PIBT and LaCAM. This approach allows for real-time replanning of up to a thousand robots in both 2D and 3D environments.

What is the key technical idea?

The TAKT framework operates in three stages: discretization, discrete coordination, and continuous reconstruction. First, the workspace is discretized into a lattice, and a safety margin is calculated based on the robot's dynamics. Second, a discrete solver (either an adapted PIBT or LaCAM) finds a collision-free path on the lattice using a diamond-shaped conflict rule to allow for efficient maneuvering. Finally, a closed-form convolution is applied to each robot's discrete plan to transform it into a smooth, continuous-time trajectory that is guaranteed to be dynamically feasible and collision-free.

How was it validated?

The researchers evaluated TAKT-PIBT and TAKT-LaCAM in 2D and 3D environments, including warehouse halls and open volumes. In 2D hall environments, the planners were over 2,000 times faster than the strongest baseline with 50 robots, and over 100 times faster in 3D. The scalability tests showed that the planners can handle approximately 1,000 robots, completing 2D plans in seconds and 3D plans in tens of seconds. Additionally, physics simulations confirmed that differential-drive robots and quadrotors could successfully track the generated trajectories without collisions.

What are the limitations and open questions?

The framework provides a mathematical guarantee of soundness, meaning any valid discrete plan can be reconstructed into a feasible continuous trajectory. However, the efficiency of the planner depends on the choice of lattice resolution and the safety margins reserved. Future work is suggested to include tighter certificates for narrow passages, the incorporation of tracking uncertainty and communication delays, and the co-design of lattice resolution and dynamic constraints to optimize solution quality and runtime.

Detailed section summary

Background

Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF) focuses on finding collision-free paths for multiple agents. Kinodynamic MAPF (KMAMPP) extends this by adding physical constraints, ensuring that the paths are executable by real robots with limited acceleration and velocity.

Limitations of prior work

The primary challenge in KMAMPP is the high dimensionality of the search space when considering the continuous states of multiple robots, which prevents real-time performance for large fleets.

Technical highlights

The diamond formulation allows robots to move sideways to yield to others in a single time step, which is more efficient than standard grid-based movement. The use of closed-form convolutions allows the reconstruction step to be computed independently for each robot, ensuring the computational complexity scales linearly with the number of agents.

Experiments

Experiments were conducted in 2D (empty halls, obstacle-filled halls, and warehouses) and 3D (open volumes and shelf volumes). Baselines included db-CBS, db-LaCAM, and Joint SST. The evaluation covered fleet sizes from 50 to 1,000 robots and included online replanning scenarios.

Results

TAKT-based planners achieved massive speedups over state-of-the-art baselines. They demonstrated the ability to replan for hundreds of robots within 500ms and scale to a thousand robots for incremental replanning, maintaining comparable or even lower makespan and cost than existing methods.

Conclusion

TAKT provides a scalable and efficient framework for kinodynamic multi-agent motion planning, enabling real-time coordination of large-scale robot fleets with guaranteed dynamic feasibility.
