In short: This paper extends the short-horizon PIBT solver for online lifelong MAPF with time-consuming rotations by selecting bounded multi-action operations rather than only one next-step action. What is new compared with prior work?

The main contribution is EPIBT, an enhanced version of PIBT that introduces bounded multi-action operations. Instead of invoking a separate path-planning routine, it evaluates short operation sequences directly, allowing the solver to look across several timesteps while retaining PIBT’s rule-based, local processing style. The method also adds agent revisiting within one step and operation inheritance, so an earlier decision can be reconsidered or an operation can be reused through the priority dependency structure. What is the key technical idea?

Original PIBT is a single-step windowed solver. It assigns priorities, described as depending on distance to the goal, and processes agents in order while avoiding collisions; when an action requires another agent to move, priority is inherited and the procedure can backtrack to try alternatives. EPIBT replaces the single-action choice with a bounded set of multi-action operations, trying an operation and reverting it if the attempt fails. Agents may be revisited during the same step even after a collision-free choice has been found, but revisiting is limited to prevent excessive computation. Operation inheritance lets an agent receive an operation inherited from another agent when its own alternatives fail. In the broader configuration, large neighborhood search uses the remaining time budget to accept solutions that improve a goal-proximity-based metric, while graph guidance is also incorporated. For the rotation model, the excerpt names five operations used to mimic an omnidirectional action model: FWW, RFW, CFW, RRF, and WWW.

Detailed section summary

Background

Classical MAPF asks a group of agents to reach assigned goals without collisions, and optimal solving becomes difficult as the number or density of agents grows. Optimal methods such as CBS and ICTS face runtime and scalability challenges on large instances, motivating fast initial-solution methods, windowed approaches, and anytime optimization. PIBT is widely used as a low-level planner or action sampler because it can select collision-free actions for thousands of agents within milliseconds while considering only the immediate next timestep. That efficiency is also the source of its weakness when an agent must spend time rotating before it can make useful progress.

Limitations of prior work

Each agent starts at a unique location and orientation on a grid whose cells may be traversable or blocked. Its state therefore includes both position and orientation, and only its currently assigned goal is visible at a given time. Once a goal is reached, the external assignment module supplies another one, producing a lifelong online routing problem. The experiments use a one-second computation budget per timestep, but the excerpt does not establish that this budget is a universal requirement of the problem definition.

Technical highlights

A multi-action operation is a short action sequence rather than a single immediate action. Increasing operation length expands the number of reachable cells, states, and distinct occupied-cell sequences, which increases the search burden. The presented evaluation uses lengths 3, 4, and 5, and the rotation-model adaptation considers five named operations to emulate omnidirectional movement that may require two or three actions. EPIBT also supports revisiting and operation inheritance, while its theoretical analysis retains the original PIBT graph assumption. The excerpt states that the original PIBT proof properties are preserved under that assumption, but it does not establish stronger guarantees for arbitrary graphs or unrestricted operation lengths.

Experiments

The first experimental component is an ablation of multi-action operations, revisiting, operation inheritance, and operation length. The authors evaluate lengths 3, 4, and 5 and examine their effects on runtime and throughput. The second component aggregates throughput across instances and compares EPIBT variants with PIBT, Causal PIBT, and configurations that add large neighborhood search or graph guidance. The final comparison evaluates EPIBT with both enhancements against other state-of-the-art approaches for online LMAPF-T. Results

The paper reports that EPIBT consistently outperforms PIBT and Causal PIBT across all five evaluated maps. Limited revisiting improves performance substantially, whereas unlimited revisiting can exhaust the time budget; operation length 3 is usually best, although longer operations sometimes help on large maps. Even with 10,000 agents, EPIBT computes the next action in under 100 milliseconds according to the excerpt. EPIBT with length 3, LNS, and graph guidance achieves a score of 1.0 on three of the five maps and is reported to outperform the alternatives in most cases on the other two. Other methods occasionally do better on random-32-32-20 and brc202d, especially at low agent counts, so the claim is not that EPIBT wins every individual instance.

Conclusion

The paper introduces EPIBT as a PIBT modification designed for rotation action models. Multi-action operations provide a limited multi-timestep lookahead without launching a separate path planner, while revisiting and operation inheritance improve the handling of local dependencies. The reported evaluation shows improved throughput with fast runtime, and the combination with LNS and graph guidance is claimed to surpass the existing methods considered for online LMAPF-T. Within the supplied evidence, this supports a strong empirical result rather than a universal optimality or completeness claim.

Limitations and open problems

The evaluation is confined to five competition-derived maps, selected goal sequences, a cyclic task-pool assignment, and the stated rotation-oriented problem setting. Generalization to other environments, task distributions, assignment policies, or action models is not established by the excerpt. Longer operations can create collisions that the current method cannot always resolve, and the theoretical properties rely on a graph-structure assumption inherited from PIBT. The excerpt also omits complete numerical results and does not quantify the contribution of every LNS or graph-guidance design choice separately.

Why it matters to MAPF researchers

The work is important because it targets a practical weakness of a highly scalable MAPF primitive without abandoning its lightweight local decision process. It offers a way to handle orientation and rotation costs while still generating actions quickly enough for large agent populations. The combination of a fast reactive core with LNS and graph guidance is relevant to warehouse logistics, transportation, and other settings where goals arrive online and computation is tightly bounded.
