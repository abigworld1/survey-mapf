In short: This paper proposes an alternative ASP approach for MAPF that uses partial orders instead of discrete time steps, enabling efficient routing and scheduling without fixed plan length bounds.

What is this paper about?

Traditional Answer Set Programming (ASP) encodings for Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF) typically rely on indexing actions and fluents with discrete time steps. While effective for simple reachability, this approach struggles when fine-grained scheduling (e.g., specific durations and deadlines) is required, as the number of variables grows linearly with the plan length. This leads to significant performance degradation as the temporal granularity increases.

What is new compared with prior work?

The authors introduce a novel way to model routing and scheduling in ASP by capturing the flow of time through partial orders on actions and/or fluents, rather than fixed time steps. This approach eliminates the need for a predefined upper bound on plan length. Furthermore, the paper demonstrates how scheduling can be outsourced to external difference constraint propagators to maintain efficiency.

What is the key technical idea?

The method involves characterizing plans as sets of ordered events. For routing, the problem is formulated in terms of event orderings that ensure collision-free movement. For scheduling, the authors extend the model to weighted graphs where edges represent travel durations. They propose several encodings, including an 'order AC' encoding using acyclicity constraints and an 'order DC' encoding that utilizes difference constraints to handle temporal dependencies efficiently.

How was it validated?

The effectiveness of the proposed encodings was evaluated using a custom ASP-based benchmark generator for grid-based MAPF problems, including random, room, and maze configurations. The experiments involved 199 instances, testing various scenarios such as vertex-conflict-free and swap-conflict-free settings. The evaluation compared the proposed 'order' encodings against a standard 'vanilla' ASP encoding on a compute cluster.

What are the limitations and open questions?

The authors note that while the partial-order approach avoids the need for fixed plan lengths, it requires temporal trajectories to be acyclic, as multiple occurrences of the same action cannot be distinguished without time steps. Additionally, fine-grained scheduling cannot be handled efficiently within ASP itself and must be delegated to external mechanisms like difference constraint propagators. The study also acknowledges that the complexity of deciding MAPF solutions can be NP-Hard even with predefined paths.

Detailed section summary

Background

Answer Set Programming (ASP) is a powerful declarative programming paradigm used for solving combinatorial problems like MAPF, phylogenetic inference, and wire routing. In MAPF, agents must navigate a graph from start to goal vertices without colliding. Traditional approaches use situation calculus or temporal logic, which rely on discrete time steps to index states and actions.

Limitations of prior work

The core problem addressed is the scalability of ASP when combining routing with scheduling. When time is represented by discrete steps, the problem size increases with the duration of the plan. This makes it difficult to represent fine-grained timings or long-duration plans without incurring massive computational overhead in the grounding and solving phases.

Technical highlights

The technical core involves defining plans as path-based ordered event sets. This allows the representation of a plan without an explicit time index. The 'order DC' encoding specifically uses difference constraints (e.g., A+1 eq B) to manage temporal relationships, which can be handled more efficiently by external propagators than by standard ASP rules.

Experiments

Experiments were conducted on three types of grid-based instances: random, room, and maze. The researchers tested the 'vanilla' encoding, 'order AC' (using acyclicity constraints), and 'order DC' (using difference constraints). The experiments were conducted on Intel Xeon CPUs with a 3-hour timeout and 16GB memory limit per instance.

Results

The results indicate that the 'vanilla' encoding is suitable for short plan lengths but fails as complexity increases. The 'order AC' encoding performs well for path-based plans in vertex-conflict-free settings but suffers from massive grounding sizes in practice. However, the 'order DC' encoding proved highly effective for path-based plans with large plan lengths, regardless of the specific conflict-handling constraints used.

Conclusion

The paper concludes that using partial orders for time representation in ASP provides a scalable alternative for MAPF. By separating the routing logic (handled by ASP) from the fine-grained scheduling (handled by difference constraints), the proposed 'order DC' approach overcomes the limitations of traditional time-indexed ASP encodings.

Limitations and open problems

A primary limitation is that the temporal trajectories must be acyclic to ensure that multiple occurrences of the same action can be distinguished. The approach also relies on the ability to interface ASP with external difference constraint solvers for efficient scheduling.

Why it matters to MAPF researchers

This work is significant for the field of automated planning and robotics, as it provides a way to solve large-scale, time-sensitive multi-agent coordination problems that were previously computationally prohibitive for ASP.
