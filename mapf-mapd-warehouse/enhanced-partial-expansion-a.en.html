In short: In high-branching search domains, generating and storing surplus nodes whose cost exceeds the optimal-solution cost can dominate runtime and memory. The paper introduces Enhanced Partial Expansion A*, or EPEA*, which uses domain- and heuristic-specific operator-selection knowledge to generate only children with the evaluation value currently needed by the search, and extends the idea to IDA* as EPEIDA*.

What is this paper about?

The paper studies optimal state-space search guided by an admissible heuristic, with particular attention to domains in which a node has many applicable operators. Standard A* generates the children of an expanded node and adds frontier candidates to OPEN, so children that are not immediately needed for an optimal solution may still consume time and memory. The paper calls nodes whose cost exceeds the optimal-solution cost surplus nodes. Partial Expansion A* avoids retaining surplus nodes in OPEN, but it still generates all children before deciding which ones are needed. EPEA* addresses that remaining generation cost by selecting appropriate operators before their children are generated.

What is new compared with prior work?

The main contribution is a refinement of Partial Expansion A* that moves partial expansion from post-generation filtering to pre-generation operator selection. EPEA* uses prior knowledge about a domain and its heuristic in an Operator Selection Function, or OSF, to identify operators producing children with the evaluation value currently required by the search. The paper studies full-checking, direct-computation, and hybrid OSFs, and develops OSFs based on pattern databases and additive pattern databases. It also extends the principle to iterative-deepening A*, producing Enhanced Partial Expansion IDA*, and provides theoretical analyses intended to explain when the additional computation is worthwhile.

What is the key technical idea?

When EPEA* expands a node, it does not immediately turn every applicable operator into a child node. Its OSF first identifies the operators whose children have the evaluation value currently required by the search. Only those children are generated and inserted into OPEN; the parent is reinserted with the next relevant value so that later children can be produced in the same search order without materializing surplus children. A full-checking OSF examines candidate operators, a direct-computation OSF derives the needed operators from domain-specific structure, and a hybrid OSF combines the two strategies. For additive pattern databases, the OSF combines contributions from several abstractions, and needed entries can be computed lazily when a node is first expanded. EPEIDA* applies analogous threshold-based selection within IDA* iterations.

The reported evaluation covers standard benchmark domains including the 15-puzzle, pancake puzzle, Rubik’s cube, single-agent pathfinding, and multi-agent pathfinding. In the MAPF study, 1,000 instances place agents on an obstacle-free, four-connected 8-by-8 grid; all variants run in an iterative-deepening framework and receive a limit of two minutes and two gigabytes of memory per instance. The MAPF comparison specifically includes EPEA* with the SIC heuristic and EPEA* with an additive-pattern-database-based OSF.

What are the limitations and open questions?

The reported results show substantial runtime and memory improvements for EPEA* and EPEIDA* on several standard benchmark applications, and the conclusion describes their runtime performance as state of the art on the reported domains. For difficult MAPF instances, EPEA* with the additive-pattern-database OSF is described as the clear winner and runs up to three times faster than EPEA* using the SIC heuristic. The advantage is not unconditional: partial expansion does not always make OPEN smaller, and a particular MAPF construction can cause PEA* and EPEA* to maintain a much larger OPEN than A*. Performance depends on average branching factor, implementation costs of ordinary A* and IDA*, OSF cost, and domain- and heuristic-dependent parameters. EPEA* is therefore a specialized optimization whose benefit depends on whether selecting operators costs less than generating and managing surplus children.

Detailed section summary

Background

A* selects frontier nodes using an evaluation formed from the cost already incurred and a heuristic estimate. With an admissible, non-overestimating heuristic, it is guaranteed to find an optimal solution; IDA* and RBFS are related state-space search methods. Partial Expansion A* saves memory by placing only children with the currently needed evaluation value into OPEN and reinserting the parent with the best discarded value. This leaves the cost of generating and inspecting all children, which is the time overhead that motivates EPEA*.

Limitations of prior work

The central problem is the surplus-node effect. A node with a large branching factor can produce many children whose evaluation values exceed the cost of an optimal solution, so they are not immediately useful to an optimal best-first search. Standard A* may nevertheless generate them and add them to OPEN, causing runtime and memory growth. PEA* removes much of the storage burden but still pays for generating every child. The challenge is to preserve the search order and optimality of partial expansion while identifying useful operators without first constructing every child.

Technical highlights

EPEA* uses an OSF to select, before child creation, the applicable operators that produce the required evaluation increment. Full-checking OSFs inspect candidate operators, direct-computation OSFs derive the needed candidates from domain-specific knowledge, and hybrid OSFs combine the approaches. Pattern databases represent abstractions that retain only selected state variables, while additive pattern databases combine contributions from multiple abstractions. In the MAPF additive-PDB OSF, needed difference-PDB entries are computed lazily at a node’s first expansion if they are not already available.

Conclusion

EPEA* advances partial expansion by using prior domain and heuristic knowledge to avoid generating surplus nodes rather than merely avoiding their storage. Its OSF mechanism can be adapted to simple local heuristics, pattern databases, additive pattern databases, and, with a related threshold interpretation, IDA*. The experiments and theoretical discussion support strong benefits when branching is large and the OSF can be computed efficiently. They also show that memory behavior, runtime, and applicability depend on structural and implementation factors. The paper therefore presents EPEA* as a domain-sensitive complement to A*, PEA*, and IDA*, not as an unconditional replacement.

Limitations and open problems

EPEA* requires sufficiently detailed domain- and heuristic-specific knowledge, and the abstract explicitly notes that it is not always applicable or practical. Constructing or evaluating an OSF can itself be expensive, especially when the branching factor is not large enough to justify that cost. Partial expansion can sometimes increase rather than decrease the size of OPEN, as illustrated by a MAPF case in the supplied material. The analysis depends on branching behavior, implementation costs, OSF cost, and EPEA*-specific parameters, but the excerpt does not provide a generally reliable way to estimate all of them. The paper also identifies an unresolved trade-off between EPEA* and exploiting the full power of value propagation with inconsistent heuristics; an experimental study of that trade-off is left for future work.
