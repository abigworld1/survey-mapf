In short: LSRP*-LA is a scalable, complete, and anytime planner for Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF) involving large agents and asynchronous actions, capable of significantly improving solution quality during runtime.

What is this paper about?

Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF) aims to find collision-free paths for multiple agents from start to goal locations while minimizing total path costs. Most existing MAPF planners rely on two simplifying assumptions: that agents are points (occupying a single vertex) and that all agents move synchronously. However, in real-world applications like automated warehouses, agents are often 'large' (occupying multiple cells) and move asynchronously (actions start and end at different times). Current planners struggle to handle both large agents and asynchronous actions simultaneously, often failing to scale or failing to improve solutions once an initial path is found.

What is new compared with prior work?

This paper introduces LSRP*-LA, a novel planner designed for Large Agent MAPF with Asynchronous Actions (LA-MAPF-AA). It addresses the scalability and solution quality issues by incorporating an 'evacuation' mechanism, which allows pushed agents to move aside and create space for large agents. LSRP*-LA is both complete and anytime, meaning it guarantees finding a solution if one exists and continues to refine that solution until the computation time budget is exhausted. It significantly outperforms existing synchronous and rule-based planners in terms of both agent capacity and solution cost.

What is the key technical idea?

The LSRP*-LA framework utilizes a search tree combined with an action tree to manage the complex state space of asynchronous movements. The action tree enumerates all valid joint actions for idle agents, ensuring that no two actions conflict in terms of swept areas or footprints. The search tree explores these joint actions to find the lowest-cost paths. To handle the complexities of large agents pushing others, the method employs a 'Push Tree' and 'Ban Chain' mechanism. This facilitates an 'evacuation' process, where agents are strategically moved to clear paths, preventing the infinite pushing cycles that often plague large-agent planners.

How was it validated?

The researchers evaluated LSRP*-LA on four diverse maps (den520d, maze-128-128-10, Berlin_1_256, and empty-48-48) with up to 800 agents. The performance was compared against five baselines: the rule-based LSRP-LA, the optimal CBS-AA-LA (extended for large agents), the prioritized PP-SIPP-LA, and two synchronous planners (MD-PIBT and Layered LA-MAPF). The results demonstrate that LSRP*-LA can handle twice as many agents as the baseline methods and achieves solution costs approximately half those of recent synchronous planners. Furthermore, it maintains a solution quality within 2% of the optimal CBS-AA-LA in small-scale instances.

What are the limitations and open questions?

The study highlights that the 'evacuation' mechanism is critical for the performance of anytime refinement in large agent scenarios; without it, pushed agents can cause massive increases in path costs. While LSRP*-LA is complete and scalable, the authors note that it does not account for agents rotating to navigate through narrow spaces when pushed. Future work is suggested to incorporate such complex maneuvers to further enhance the planner's capabilities in constrained environments.

Detailed section summary

Background

MAPF is an NP-hard problem central to logistics and automated warehouse management. As the number of agents and the complexity of their shapes increase, the computational difficulty grows exponentially, necessitating a balance between scalability and solution optimality.

Limitations of prior work

The core problem addressed is LA-MAPF-AA, where agents occupy multiple cells and actions are not synchronized. This combination makes finding even a feasible solution NP-hard and creates a massive search space for joint actions, as agents can start and end their movements at any time.

Technical highlights

The method defines a 'joint action' as a set of actions for all agents occurring at a common time event, which is the earliest arrival time among active agents. It also introduces a 'strict evacuation' condition to ensure that the evacuation process effectively clears a path without causing new conflicts.

Experiments

Experiments were conducted on a MacBook Air (Apple M4) with a 60-second time limit per instance. The agents were modeled as 2x2 or 3x3 squares, with approximately 65% of the agents being larger than a single cell to reflect realistic warehouse conditions.

Results

LSRP*-LA demonstrated superior scalability, handling up to 1000 agents in certain map configurations. In terms of solution cost, it was significantly more efficient than synchronous planners, which ignore the benefits of asynchronous timing. The anytime property was validated by showing that the solution cost decreases significantly as the runtime increases, especially when evacuation is enabled.

Conclusion

LSRP*-LA provides a robust, scalable, and anytime solution for the challenging LA-MAPF-AA problem, bridging the gap between theoretical completeness and practical scalability for large, asynchronous agents.

Limitations and open problems

The current implementation does not consider agent rotation as a means to navigate narrow passages when being pushed.

Why it matters to MAPF researchers

This work is highly relevant for real-world robotics applications where agents have physical dimensions and operate on independent, asynchronous schedules.

Who should read this paper

Researchers and engineers working on large-scale multi-robot coordination in logistics, automated warehouses, and complex spatial environments.
