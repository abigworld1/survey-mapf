What is this paper about?

The target problem is multi-robot motion planning (MRMP) in a shared continuous planar workspace. Multiple robots must move from assigned start positions to goal positions while avoiding static obstacles and one another and respecting a first-order kinematic speed bound. Discrete multi-agent path finding (MAPF) scales comparatively well, but its coarse grid representation can produce low-quality trajectories and does not directly enforce continuous-space separation. Continuous optimization can produce smooth, high-quality paths, but its nonconvexity and high dimensionality make it difficult to scale as the number of robots and obstacles grows. The paper addresses this tension by using a discrete solution as structure rather than as the final continuous trajectory.

What is the key technical idea?

DGD first partitions the nonconvex continuous problem into multiple local regions, reducing each subproblem to a lower-dimensional space. MAPF is used to produce a spatiotemporal skeleton: for each robot and subproblem, it provides structural information about local starts, goals, and the temporal dependencies among robots. A diffusion model then generates continuous trajectories conditioned on this structure and on constrained-optimization guidance, allowing it to model interactions among robots without forcing the whole problem onto a very fine grid. During diffusion, a projected or constraint-aware guidance step repairs generated trajectories when violations occur, targeting obstacle avoidance, inter-robot separation, and the speed limit. The repair process is described as lightweight and is further simplified through Lagrangian relaxation, avoiding expensive global projection operations.

Detailed section summary

Background

MRMP research includes sampling-based, optimization-based, and discrete MAPF-based approaches. Sampling methods can be probabilistically complete, but they may require dense sampling and have difficulty enforcing continuous kinodynamic constraints. Optimization methods can produce smooth paths but become difficult to solve as nonconvexity and the number of degrees of freedom increase. MAPF avoids some of this complexity through discretization, yet coarse grids limit geometric and temporal fidelity while fine grids cause rapid state-space growth. Recent diffusion models generate diverse, high-quality continuous trajectories, but ensuring feasibility over an entire multi-robot trajectory, especially under inter-robot interactions, remains difficult.

Limitations of prior work

A discrete MAPF solution must be converted into continuous motion while preserving collision avoidance, time parameterization, and the first-order speed constraint. Coarse cells can lead to unrealistic motion or infeasible timing, whereas finer cells make the discrete problem expensive. MAPF normally resolves vertex and edge conflicts, but those conditions do not by themselves guarantee continuous-space separation between moving robots. DGD therefore treats MAPF as a structural prior for continuous generation rather than as a complete trajectory plan.

Technical highlights

Each robot is modeled as a disk with a radius, and its trajectory is represented as a sequence of two-dimensional positions at discrete time steps. The central constraints are avoidance of static obstacles, sufficient separation from other robots, and a speed bound determined by the time step. DGD combines local convex subproblems, a MAPF-derived spatiotemporal skeleton, diffusion-based generation, and conditional constraint repair. Repair is applied when violations are detected, with the intended benefit of reducing the cost of projecting the entire high-dimensional problem at every step.
