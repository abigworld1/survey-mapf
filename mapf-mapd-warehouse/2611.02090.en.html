In short: This paper introduces Local Solution Repair (LSR), a runtime approach that detects and repairs suboptimal agent behaviors in MAPD solutions to improve system throughput with minimal computational overhead.

What is this paper about?

In automated warehouses, multi-robot systems must handle a continuous stream of pickup-and-delivery tasks while avoiding collisions. Current Multi-Agent Pickup-and-Delivery (MAPD) solutions often prioritize scalability by decoupling task allocation from motion planning, which leads to suboptimal runtime behaviors. These inefficiencies, such as robots backtracking from goals or idling in congested aisles, accumulate over long-horizon deployments and significantly degrade overall system throughput.

What is new compared with prior work?

The authors propose Local Solution Repair (LSR), a framework that identifies subsets of agents exhibiting suboptimal behavior and reassigns their goals to improve efficiency. The work demonstrates that performance can be improved by repairing existing plans rather than recomputing them from scratch. Specifically, the paper shows that LSR can enhance the throughput of state-of-the-art baselines like M2M and LNS-PBS by up to 5.9% while reducing suboptimal actions significantly.

What is the key technical idea?

LSR operates through three main stages: suboptimality detection, agent grouping, and goal reassignment. Once suboptimal agents are identified and grouped, the method employs two repair variants: Estimated-Cost Repair (ECR) and MAPF-Cost Repair (MAPF-CR). ECR uses estimated path costs and the Hungarian Algorithm for rapid reassignment, while MAPF-CR uses a Branch-and-Bound search to ensure collision-free, optimal paths. A repair is only accepted if it results in a lower total path cost than the original plan.

How was it validated?

The method was validated through long-horizon, 8-hour warehouse simulations across three distinct 2D layouts: Restricted, Open-Top, and Open. The study compared LSR applied to two baselines, M2M and LNS-PBS, against their original versions. Results showed that LSR improves throughput by up to 5.9% and reduces suboptimal actions by up to 14.6% (approximately 36,000 instances) compared to current state-of-the-art methods.

What are the limitations and open questions?

The results indicate a trade-off between computational time and performance gains; MAPF-CR provides higher throughput but requires more time than ECR. The effectiveness of LSR is also dependent on the environment; it is more impactful in narrow, restricted aisles where conflicts are more likely to cause backtracking. The authors note that the method is designed to be lightweight, maintaining a computational budget of approximately 1 second per repair cycle.

Detailed section summary

Background

MAPD is an NP-hard problem involving the coordination of multiple agents to fulfill tasks. It is often treated as a combination of Multi-Robot Task Allocation (MRTA) and Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF).

Limitations of prior work

Decoupling task allocation and motion planning allows for scalability but results in 'suboptimal instances' where agents perform inefficient movements due to a lack of awareness of future collisions or task dependencies.

Technical highlights

The approach focuses on the Suboptimality Detection and Repair (SDR) problem. It utilizes a grouping mechanism to isolate conflicting agents and employs a cost-based acceptance criterion to ensure that repairs never degrade the existing solution.

Experiments

Experiments were conducted in simulated warehouse environments with 27x50 grids. The study used three map types to test the robustness of the repair mechanism across different spatial constraints.

Results

LSR + MAPF-CR achieved the highest throughput gains, particularly when applied to the LNS-PBS baseline. The reduction in suboptimal actions was most pronounced in the Restricted and Open-Top maps, where narrow aisles exacerbate the impact of poor task allocation.

Conclusion

LSR provides a practical and efficient way to improve MAPD performance by addressing inefficiencies at runtime. It proves that scalability and optimality do not require a complete overhaul of existing solvers but can be achieved through lightweight, local repairs.

Limitations and open problems

The method's effectiveness is limited by the size of the agent group, which is capped to ensure computational feasibility. Additionally, the performance gains vary depending on the warehouse layout's connectivity.

Why it matters to MAPF researchers

This work is significant for real-world warehouse logistics where maintaining high throughput and minimizing robot idling is critical for operational efficiency.
