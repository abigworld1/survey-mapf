In short: This paper proposes VR-CBS, a new algorithm for smooth continuous multi-agent path finding (SC-MAPF) that utilizes local visibility roadmaps to achieve efficient and smooth trajectories.

What is this paper about?

Multi-agent path finding (MAPF) is essential for navigating multiple agents in shared environments without collisions. While traditional MAPF often relies on discrete graph-based models, real-world applications like UAV swarms and warehouse robots require continuous time and space, as well as smooth trajectories to accommodate kinematic constraints and maintain speed during turns.

What is new compared with prior work?

The authors introduce Visibility Roadmap Conflict-Based Search (VR-CBS), which integrates a high-level CBS-based conflict resolution framework with a low-level single-agent path planner using visibility roadmaps. Unlike previous sampling-based approaches like CE-CBS that use RRT*, VR-CBS uses A* on visibility roadmaps, leading to improved path quality (lower Sum of Costs) and reduced computational time. Additionally, the method incorporates B-spline smoothing to ensure trajectories are suitable for real robots.

What is the key technical idea?

VR-CBS operates on two levels. At the high level, it employs a CBS-like search to resolve agent collisions by imposing constraints. At the low level, for each agent, a visibility roadmap is constructed considering static obstacles and current collision constraints (treated as new static obstacles). The algorithm performs A* search on these roadmaps to find optimal paths, which are subsequently smoothed using B-spline curves to eliminate sharp turns and ensure smoothness.

How was it validated?

The performance of VR-CBS was evaluated against CE-CBS (which uses RRT* for low-level planning) and SMT-CCBS (a SAT-based solver for continuous time). Experiments were conducted on various scenarios, including obstacle-free maps, mazes, and environments requiring agents to swap positions. The evaluation used the Sum of Costs (SOC) metric to measure path quality and compared execution times across different map instances.

What are the limitations and open questions?

The results demonstrate that VR-CBS outperforms CE-CBS in both path quality and computational efficiency, largely because it avoids the stochastic nature of sampling. The algorithm also successfully navigates narrow corridors. However, the authors note that the current approach does not yet optimize curve parameters for specific kinematic properties like maximum turn rate, and future work will address safety-aware optimization and kinematic integration.

Detailed section summary

Background

Smooth Continuous MAPF (SC-MAPF) involves planning paths in a metric space where agents move along smooth polynomial curves in continuous time. A conflict occurs when the bodies of two agents overlap at a specific time and location. Resolving these conflicts requires finding alternative trajectories that avoid the conflicting space-time regions.

Limitations of prior work

The core problem is to find a set of collision-free, smooth trajectories for multiple agents in a continuous environment. This requires balancing the need for efficient global coordination (to avoid deadlocks) with the need for local smoothness and kinematic feasibility.

Technical highlights

The key technical innovation is the use of per-agent visibility roadmaps that are dynamically updated. When a conflict is detected, the conflicting region is added to the agent's roadmap as a new obstacle, and the roadmap is updated via local line-of-sight checks, allowing for efficient re-planning using A*.

Experiments

Experiments were performed on three types of scenarios to compare VR-CBS with CE-CBS: an obstacle-free map, a maze, and a 'switch' scenario. Furthermore, VR-CBS was tested against SMT-CCBS on 8 instances from the DAO grid map dataset, which were converted into continuous environments. The tests included varying numbers of agents from 1 to 10.

Results

Experimental results show that VR-CBS achieves a lower Sum of Costs (SOC) than both CE-CBS and SMT-CCBS across all tested instances. For example, in the 'switch' scenario, VR-CBS achieved a lower SOC than CE-CBS. The algorithm also demonstrated the ability to navigate through narrow corridors effectively.

Conclusion

The paper concludes that VR-CBS is a highly effective algorithm for SC-MAPF, providing smoother and shorter paths more quickly than existing sampling-based or SAT-based continuous MAPF methods.

Limitations and open problems

The study does not explicitly address the combinatorial optimization of B-spline parameters to perfectly match an agent's specific kinematic constraints, such as velocity or acceleration limits. Additionally, the integration of user-defined safety requirements into the optimization process is identified as a future direction.

Why it matters to MAPF researchers

This work is significant for the field of multi-robot motion planning, providing a bridge between high-level discrete coordination and low-level continuous trajectory generation for real-world robotic systems.

Who should read this paper

Researchers working on multi-agent coordination, continuous motion planning, and the application of MAPF to real-world robotic platforms.
