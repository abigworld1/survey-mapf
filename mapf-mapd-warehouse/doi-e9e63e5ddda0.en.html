What is this paper about?

Multi-Agent Pathfinding (MAPF) is the problem of planning paths for multiple agents so that each reaches its target and all paths can be executed concurrently without collisions. Motivating applications include automated warehouses, autonomous vehicles, and robotics. In classical MAPF, time is discrete, agents occupy graph vertices, and each agent performs one move or wait action per time step. Existing papers make different assumptions about motion and conflicts, including whether agents may traverse the same road at the same time, and optimize different objectives such as makespan or the sum of action costs. Because these assumptions and objectives may be implicit or described with inconsistent terminology, it is difficult to understand the literature, select appropriate baselines, or find work matching a concrete application.
