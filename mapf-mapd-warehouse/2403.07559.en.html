What is this paper about?

Multi-Agent Pathfinding (MAPF) is the task of finding collision-free paths for multiple agents in a map with obstacles, aiming to minimize costs like makespan. While classical search-based solvers like Conflict-Based Search (CBS) are effective, they struggle to scale as the number of agents increases due to exponential complexity. Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) approaches offer scalability but face significant challenges in structured environments with high obstacle density and high agent counts, particularly when agents remain at their targets and act as obstacles.

What is new compared with prior work?

The authors propose Ensembling Prioritized Hybrid Policies (EPH), a novel MARL-based MAPF solver. The primary contribution is the integration of a selective communication block using graph convolutions to enrich agent coordination. Furthermore, the work introduces three distinct inference-time strategies: hybridizing neural policies with single-agent expert guidance, using Q-values to prioritize conflict and deadlock resolution, and employing an ensemble method to select the best solution from multiple parallel solvers. This approach allows EPH to achieve competitive performance against state-of-the-art neural MAPF methods in complex environments.

What is the key technical idea?

EPH is formulated as a Q-learning problem and trained using the Double Dueling Deep Q Network (D3QN) algorithm with prioritized experience replay and curriculum learning. The architecture includes a graph convolution-based selective communication block to facilitate information exchange among agents. During the inference phase, the method employs three specific strategies: first, it hybridizes the neural policy with single-agent expert guidance for navigating conflict-free zones; second, it utilizes Q-values to prioritize the resolution of conflicts and deadlocks; third, it uses an ensemble method to sample the best solution from multiple parallel solvers to leverage their diverse strengths.

How was it validated?

The performance of EPH was empirically evaluated in complex, structured 2D grid-world environments, specifically using the 'den312d' and 'warehouse' benchmark maps. The evaluation metrics used are Episode Length (EL) and Success Rate (SR). The method was compared against several state-of-the-art baselines, including classical heuristic solvers (CBS, ODrM*) and neural MARL-based solvers (PRIMAL, DHC, DCC, SACHA, SCRIMP). The results demonstrate that EPH maintains high success rates and competitive episode lengths across various agent counts and obstacle densities.

What are the limitations and open questions?

The results indicate that EPH is highly effective in structured environments, outperforming several neural baselines. However, the authors note that EPH incurs a slightly higher runtime compared to the DCC method. The ablation study reveals that the effectiveness of the hybrid expert guidance depends on the specific environment and agent density. The authors suggest that future work could involve training with on-policy algorithms or developing more sophisticated hybridization techniques with low-level solvers to further avoid deadlocks in highly structured settings.

Detailed section summary

Background

MAPF is a critical problem in robotics, aviation, and gaming. While centralized search-based algorithms provide optimal or bounded-suboptimal solutions, they often fail to scale to large-scale problems. MARL-based approaches have emerged as a scalable alternative, utilizing partial observations and communication to manage agent coordination.

Limitations of prior work

Existing MARL-based MAPF solvers struggle to maintain high success rates in structured environments characterized by high obstacle density and high agent counts, often leading to deadlocks or collisions.

Technical highlights

Key technical elements include Graph Convolutional communication, Double Dueling Deep Q Networks (D3QN), Q-value-based conflict prioritization, hybrid expert guidance, and ensemble-based inference.

Experiments

Experiments were conducted on 'den312d' and 'warehouse' maps with varying agent counts (4 to 64) and obstacle densities. Training involved curriculum learning starting from a single agent. Performance was measured via Success Rate and Episode Length.

Results

EPH demonstrated competitive performance against state-of-the-art neural MAPF solvers, showing high success rates even in complex, high-density scenarios where other neural methods failed.

Conclusion

EPH successfully addresses the limitations of current MARL-MAPF solvers by enhancing communication and introducing robust inference-time strategies, making it highly effective for complex, structured multi-agent environments.

Limitations and open problems

The method exhibits a slightly higher computational runtime compared to the DCC baseline, and the impact of hybrid guidance varies by environment.

Why it matters to MAPF researchers

This work provides a robust framework for scalable and reliable multi-agent coordination in complex, real-world-like structured environments.
