In short: Goal-Branching enhances LaCAM for dense multi-agent pathfinding by introducing temporary goals for blocking agents as new search branches, significantly improving success rates and solution speed in corridor-heavy environments.

What is this paper about?

In Multi-Agent Pathfinding (MAPF), high-density environments with narrow corridors and dead ends present significant challenges. Agents often become deadlocked, blocking each other's progress. While the LaCAM solver is complete, it struggles in these settings because its underlying configuration generator, PIBT, typically proposes greedy, one-step moves. These moves rarely include the coordinated, non-greedy movements (such as moving away from a goal) required to resolve a deadlock, forcing LaCAM to rely on a slow, systematic enumeration of single-agent constraints to find alternatives.

What is new compared with prior work?

This paper introduces Goal-Branching, a novel mechanism that integrates temporary goals into the LaCAM search process. When a deadlock (jam) is detected, the method assigns candidate temporary goals to the blocking agents and treats each goal as a new branch in the search tree. This allows the solver to explore coordinated escape maneuvers more directly than the standard constraint-adding approach. Crucially, the authors prove that this method preserves the completeness of the LaCAM solver, regardless of the rule used to select temporary goals.

What is the key technical idea?

Goal-Branching is triggered when the PIBT step in LaCAM results in a configuration that has already been explored (a jam). Instead of merely adding a single-agent constraint, the algorithm assigns temporary goals to the agents involved in the jam. These goals are selected using either a random rule or a corridor-aware rule. Each temporary goal is then explored as a separate successor of the current node in the search tree. Each branch runs a PIBT step under these temporary goals, and the resulting configuration is added to the search tree before the standard constraint-based successors are explored.

How was it validated?

The authors evaluated Goal-Branching on six maps from the MovingAI benchmark, including bottleneck maps (mazes and rooms) and open maps. They tested densities ranging from 60% to 95% occupancy. Results on dense bottleneck maps showed that Goal-Branching solved 89% of instances, compared to only 44% for LaCAM*. In instances where both solvers found a solution, Goal-Branching was 10-14x faster on maze and room maps, and nearly 200x faster in median time at 75% density on the room-32-32-4 map. On open maps and the full MovingAI benchmark, the method was at most 3% slower and maintained solution costs comparable to LaCAM*.

What are the limitations and open questions?

The results demonstrate that Goal-Branching is highly effective for dense, corridor-heavy instances, significantly increasing the success rate and speed of finding the first solution. A trade-off is observed in solution quality: the first solutions found by Goal-Branching can be 15-22% more costly than those of LaCAM* on instances both solve. The study also notes that the 'corridor rule' produces cheaper solutions than the 'random rule' but is slower. Furthermore, the authors observe that good temporary goals often need to be far from the blocking agent to be effective. Future work includes developing more intelligent goal-selection heuristics to reduce solution costs and integrating Goal-Branching with move-preference methods like hindrance.

Detailed section summary

Background

MAPF involves finding collision-free paths for multiple agents on a graph. LaCAM is a scalable, complete solver that uses a depth-first search over joint configurations, utilizing PIBT to generate successors and adding constraints to explore alternatives when jams occur.

Limitations of prior work

Standard LaCAM struggles in dense, corridor-heavy environments because PIBT's greedy moves cannot easily propose the coordinated, non-goal-oriented moves needed to resolve deadlocks, leading to inefficient search through single-agent constraint enumeration.

Technical highlights

Goal-Branching inserts informed branches into the search tree by assigning temporary goals to agents in a jam. This bypasses the need to explore the constraint tree one agent at a time for every possible move required to resolve a deadlock.

Experiments

Experiments were conducted on AMD Ryzen 5 7600X using C++ implementations. Maps included three bottleneck maps (maze-32-32-2, maze-32-32-4, room-32-32-4) and three open maps. Densities were varied from 60% to 95% in 5% increments, with 5 random seeds per scenario.

Results

Goal-Branching achieved an 89% success rate on dense bottleneck maps, significantly outperforming LaCAM* (44%) and LaCAM3 (33%). It provided much faster first solutions in dense settings and matched the performance of LaCAM* on open maps with minimal overhead.

Conclusion

Goal-Branching is a robust extension for LaCAM that preserves completeness while drastically improving performance in dense, constrained environments by using temporary goals as search branches.

Limitations and open problems

The method can lead to a 15-22% increase in solution cost in some dense scenarios and may suffer from local optima where a rule cannot 'see' past a pusher agent.

Why it matters to MAPF researchers

This work provides a practical and theoretically sound method for improving the scalability and success rate of MAPF solvers in highly congested environments.

Who should read this paper

Researchers and practitioners working on large-scale, high-density multi-robot coordination in constrained environments like automated warehouses.
