In short: This paper proves that finding a makespan-optimal solution for Unlabeled Multi-agent Pathfinding with proximity constraints (rCUMAPF) is NP-hard, even when the proximity range r is relatively large.

What is this paper about?

The paper investigates r-Connected Unlabeled Multi-agent Pathfinding (rCUMAPF), a variant of the standard Multi-Agent Pathfinding (MAPF) problem. In this variant, agents are interchangeable (unlabeled) and must maintain a proximity range r, meaning the subgraph induced by the agents' positions must remain connected via edges between vertices at distance at most r at all times. This constraint is motivated by real-world applications like autonomous platooning or collaborative exploration where agents must maintain communication or sensing range. While the complexity of makespan-optimal rCUMAPF is known for the extreme cases of r=1 and very large r, the complexity for intermediate values of r has remained an open question.

What is new compared with prior work?

The primary contribution is the proof that finding a solution to rCUMAPF with a makespan of at most 2 is NP-hard for proximity ranges r = O(|V|^1-varepsilon), where |V| is the number of vertices and varepsilon is a fixed constant. This result fills a significant gap in the complexity classification of rCUMAPF by showing that even 'loose' proximity constraints do not make the makespan-optimal problem easy. Furthermore, the authors extend this result to show that the labeled version of the problem (CMAPF) is also NP-hard under the same conditions.

What is the key technical idea?

The authors employ a polynomial-time reduction from the 3-SAT problem to rCUMAPF. They construct a graph consisting of several specialized components: variable gadgets, clause gadgets, a connection gadget, and occurrence gadgets. The construction is designed such that a satisfying assignment for the 3-SAT formula exists if and only if there is a valid rCUMAPF plan with a makespan of at most 2. The proof involves demonstrating that the resulting graph is polynomial in size and that the proximity and collision constraints correctly simulate the logical requirements of the SAT instance.

How was it validated?

The validation is performed through rigorous mathematical proofs rather than empirical experiments. The authors prove several propositions to ensure the correctness of their reduction, specifically verifying that the constructed graph maintains a maximum degree of 3, that the initial and target configurations are r-connected, and that the size of the graph is polynomial relative to the input formula. These theoretical properties ensure that the reduction is valid and that the NP-hardness result holds.

What are the limitations and open questions?

The paper concludes that proximity constraints significantly increase the complexity of makespan-optimal pathfinding. While the results cover a large portion of the complexity landscape, a gap remains for the specific case where r is exactly at the boundary of tractability. Additionally, the reduction used does not preclude the existence of subexponential-time algorithms, which remains an area for future investigation. The authors suggest that developing such algorithms could be a valuable direction for future research.

Detailed section summary

Background

Multi-Agent Pathfinding (MAPF) is a fundamental problem in robotics and logistics, focusing on finding collision-free paths for multiple agents on a graph. Standard MAPF focuses on avoiding vertex and swap collisions. However, practical scenarios often require additional constraints, such as maintaining connectivity for communication or sensing, which leads to the study of proximity-constrained variants like rCUMAPF.

Limitations of prior work

The core problem is to determine the computational complexity of finding a makespan-optimal solution for rCUMAPF. Specifically, the authors aim to address whether the problem becomes easy (polynomial-time solvable) when the proximity range r is increased beyond the highly restrictive r=1 case, but before it becomes trivial due to very large r.

Technical highlights

The reduction relies on mapping Boolean variables to specific configurations in the graph and clauses to constraints that can only be satisfied if the agents move in a way that reflects a valid truth assignment. The proximity constraint r is used to enforce the logical consistency of these assignments across the graph structure.

Experiments

No empirical experiments or numerical simulations are presented in this paper, as it is a theoretical work focused on computational complexity. The 'results' are expressed as mathematical theorems and proofs regarding the hardness of the problem.

Results

The main result is Theorem 1, which states that for 2 <= r = O(|V|^1-varepsilon), deciding if there is a solution to rCUMAPF with makespan at most 2 is NP-hard. This implies that the problem remains computationally difficult even when the proximity requirement is relatively loose.

Conclusion

The study demonstrates that even loose proximity constraints make the makespan-optimal unlabeled MAPF problem NP-hard. This finding highlights the inherent difficulty of planning for multi-agent systems that must maintain connectivity, providing a theoretical foundation for understanding the limits of efficient pathfinding in such constrained environments.

Limitations and open problems

The hardness result is established for a very small makespan (at most 2), which is a specific subset of the makespan-optimization problem. The paper does not explore the complexity for larger makespans or the exact boundary between polynomial and NP-hard regimes. Furthermore, the possibility of subexponential-time algorithms is not ruled out by the current reduction.

This work is important for the theoretical understanding of multi-robot coordination. It provides a lower bound on the complexity of solving proximity-constrained pathfinding problems, which is crucial for researchers designing algorithms for autonomous platooning, swarm robotics, and other collaborative multi-agent systems.
