In short: This paper proposes a decentralized Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) method integrated with a lightweight learned policy to solve the Lifelong Multi-Agent Pathfinding (MAPF) problem in partially observable environments.

What is this paper about?

Multi-Agent Pathfinding (MAPF) involves finding conflict-free paths for multiple agents on a graph. While centralized algorithms work well when the full state is known, real-world scenarios often involve partial observability, where agents only see their immediate surroundings and have limited communication. This study specifically addresses the 'lifelong' variant of MAPF, where agents continuously receive new goals upon completing previous ones.

What is new compared with prior work?

The authors introduce MATS-LP, a decentralized MCTS-based solver inspired by the AlphaZero approach. The novelty lies in using an agent's local observations to reconstruct an intrinsic Markov decision process (IMDP), which is then explored using a neural MCTS tailored for multi-agent tasks. Furthermore, the method achieves state-of-the-art performance while using significantly fewer parameters than existing learnable solvers like Primal2 and SCRIMP.

What is the key technical idea?

The MATS-LP method combines MCTS with a learned policy to enable effective reasoning under uncertainty. Each agent uses its local observations to build an IMDP and performs MCTS to select actions that maximize goal achievement while minimizing collisions and deadlocks. The simulation step within the MCTS uses a lightweight neural network policy, pre-trained via Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). The final action is determined by selecting the most visited edge from the root of the search tree.

How was it validated?

The method was evaluated on both random obstacle maps and complex maze-like environments. The researchers compared MATS-LP against two state-of-the-art learnable approaches: Primal2 and SCRIMP (local version). The experiments utilized 4-connected grids with a 5x5 field-of-view, and the results demonstrated that MATS-LP outperforms the baselines in terms of average throughput across various obstacle densities.

What are the limitations and open questions?

The proposed approach is decentralized and does not require explicit communication between agents, making it suitable for constrained environments. A significant advantage is its computational efficiency; the model contains only 161,000 parameters, allowing it to be trained on a single GPU in less than an hour. Future work could explore fully learnable MCTS frameworks where the simulation policy is learned through the MCTS process itself.

Detailed section summary

Background

MAPF is a fundamental problem in robotics and logistics, such as in automated warehouses. Lifelong MAPF extends the classical problem by introducing a continuous stream of tasks. Traditional learning-based solvers often rely on high-dimensional neural networks or explicit communication to resolve deadlocks, which can be computationally expensive or impractical in decentralized settings.

Limitations of prior work

In partially observable MAPF, agents lack global knowledge of other agents' positions and goals. This information gap can lead to congestion and deadlocks, especially in narrow corridors or bottlenecks. The challenge is to develop a planning mechanism that allows agents to cooperate and navigate effectively using only local, imperfect information.

Technical highlights

The method utilizes the Probabilistic Upper Confidence Bound applied to the Tree (PUCT) to balance exploration and exploitation during MCTS. The agent's decision-making is based on an IMDP constructed from local observations. The architecture uses a relatively compact neural network to maintain efficiency while providing high-quality action distributions for the tree search.

Experiments

Experiments were conducted using 40 random maps and 50 maze-like maps for each of three different sizes. The field-of-view for agents was set to 5x5, and the episode length was 1000 steps. The comparison was performed against the local version of SCRIMP and Primal2, using their official implementations and weights.

Results

The empirical results show that MATS-LP achieves higher average throughput than both Primal2 and SCRIMP in various scenarios. The performance advantage is particularly notable in environments with varying obstacle densities, proving the robustness of the decentralized MCTS approach.

Conclusion

The study concludes that a decentralized MCTS equipped with a lightweight learned policy is highly effective for solving the Lifelong MAPF problem under partial observability. The method generalizes well to unseen instances and outperforms current state-of-the-art learnable solvers.
