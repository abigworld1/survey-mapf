In short: The paper proposes TACT, an iterative TAPF method that improves target assignment quality by calibrating travel cost estimates using feedback from fast, suboptimal MAPF solvers.

What is this paper about?

Target Assignment and Pathfinding (TAPF) extends Multi-Agent Pathfinding (MAPF) by requiring the planner to both assign targets to agents and plan collision-free paths. A common but suboptimal approach is to use single-agent shortest-path distances for assignment, which fails to account for agent interference and the resulting delays during path execution.

What is new compared with prior work?

The authors introduce TACT, a novel iterative framework that treats the cost matrix as a surrogate and uses fast MAPF solvers as low-fidelity evaluators. This approach allows for the calibration of cost estimates based on actual observed travel costs, including interference-induced delays. The method demonstrates superior solution quality in large-scale instances compared to existing state-of-the-art solvers.

What is the key technical idea?

TACT begins by initializing the cost matrix with shortest-path distances. In each iteration, the method adds random noise to these estimates to generate diverse candidate target assignments. These assignments are then evaluated using a fast, suboptimal MAPF solver (such as LaCAM). The actual travel costs observed during path planning are fed back to update and calibrate the cost matrix, effectively incorporating agent interference into the assignment process.

How was it validated?

The method was evaluated on various MAPF benchmark maps using two scenarios: EQ-DIST (uniform target distribution) and HOTSPOT (dense target clustering). Experiments were conducted on instances with up to 800 agents. TACT was compared against several baselines, including ITA-ECBS, DBS-Hungarian, and a trivial SSP-LaCAM3 approach, using the flowtime/LB metric.

What are the limitations and open questions?

The effectiveness of TACT relies on the balance between exploration (via noise) and exploitation (via cost calibration). The study finds that excessive noise leads to near-random assignments, while insufficient noise reduces the diversity needed to escape local optima. Additionally, the method's performance is influenced by the number of parallel evaluations and the scale of the noise applied.

Detailed section summary

Background

MAPF is a fundamental problem in multi-agent systems, such as automated warehouses, where agents must navigate a shared graph without colliding. TAPF adds the complexity of task assignment, where each agent has a set of potential targets to be assigned.

Limitations of prior work

The primary problem addressed is the mismatch between the proxy costs used during target assignment (shortest-path distances) and the actual costs incurred during path execution due to agent-agent interference.

Technical highlights

The method utilizes a multi-fidelity optimization concept where expensive, high-fidelity evaluations are replaced by fast, low-fidelity MAPF solvers. It employs a stochastic approach to ensure the diversity of target assignments during the iterative refinement process.

Experiments

Experiments were performed on a Mac Studio with an M1 Ultra processor. The researchers used four-connected grid maps and tested the scalability of the method up to 800 agents in complex environments.

Results

In the HOTSPOT scenario, TACT significantly outperforms interference-agnostic assignment methods. In large-scale instances, TACT achieves better solution quality than the state-of-the-art DBS-Hungarian solver and shows high scalability for massive-scale TAPF problems.

Conclusion

TACT successfully bridges the gap between target assignment and pathfinding by iteratively refining cost estimates through real-world feedback. This results in high-quality solutions for large-scale TAPF instances that were previously computationally prohibitive.

Limitations and open problems

The paper notes that the performance is sensitive to the noise scale and the number of parallel evaluations. The authors do not explicitly detail the optimal noise schedule or the computational overhead of the calibration process in all scenarios.

Why it matters to MAPF researchers

Optimizing TAPF is critical for the efficiency of large-scale robotic logistics, where minimizing total travel time directly impacts system throughput.

Who should read this paper

Researchers working on multi-agent coordination, robotic task allocation, and large-scale pathfinding optimization.
