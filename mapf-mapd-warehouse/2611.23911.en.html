In short: This paper proposes PIBT-T*, an efficient priority-inheritance-based framework for large-scale multi-agent path finding that incorporates realistic robot rotation constraints.

What is this paper about?

In large-scale multi-mobile-robot systems (MMRSs), such as automated warehouses, robots must rotate toward a desired direction before moving forward. Traditional Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF) models often abstract away these orientation and turning constraints to simplify computation. However, this abstraction can lead to significant discrepancies between planned paths and actual robot movements, resulting in execution-level inefficiencies or deadlocks in confined spaces.

What is new compared with prior work?

The authors introduce PIBT-T*, a robust and scalable priority-inheritance framework specifically designed for MAPF with turn constraints (MAPF-T). The primary contribution is the extension of the lightweight PIBT mechanism to handle orientation-aware actions and the introduction of several specialized mechanisms: cycle handling for circular request chains, a Swap Pattern Detector (SPD) for local deadlock resolution, and a k-Push Escape Trigger (PET) to mitigate livelocks and inefficient repeated pushing. These additions allow the algorithm to maintain high success rates and scalability in large-scale, complex environments.

What is the key technical idea?

PIBT-T* operates on a single-timestep planning paradigm where agents are processed sequentially based on their priorities. Each agent chooses from three possible actions: moving forward, rotating 90 degrees (clockwise or counter-clockwise), or waiting. To address the complexities introduced by turns, the method incorporates a recursive priority inheritance procedure and a turn-aware node selection strategy. The framework also includes dedicated logic for handling circular dependencies and escaping livelocks through the PET mechanism. Cell reservation is separated from action execution: a robot that must turn keeps occupying its current cell even after its request succeeds. When repeated pushes between the same pair reach a threshold, the planner shuffles the candidate destinations.

How was it validated?

The method was evaluated through six experimental sets, covering threshold calibration, ablations, benchmark comparison, stress testing, execution simulation and a manufacturing case study. Experiments were conducted on five standard MovingAI benchmark maps with agent counts ranging from 5 to 100 in increments of five, using 25 scenarios per map and agent count and a 60-second cutoff. The scalability was tested using a large-scale stress test with up to 1,000 agents on a warehouse map. Finally, the practical feasibility was validated through a real-robot demonstration using three TurtleBot4 robots. All three robots reached their goals with the proposed method, whereas two deadlocked under classical PIBT with the same execution controller.

What are the limitations and open questions?

While PIBT-T* demonstrates high scalability and success rates, the paper notes that preprocessing time for creating cost tables increases with map size and agent density. The method's performance is also sensitive to the threshold parameter in the PET mechanism. Future work includes integrating learning-based mechanisms to optimize node selection and expanding the framework to handle dynamic tasks, uncertain execution times, and real-time environmental disturbances.

Detailed section summary

Background

Multi-agent path finding is a fundamental problem in coordinating autonomous agents. While classical MAPF focuses on vertex and edge collisions, real-world robots have kinematic constraints, most notably the need to rotate before moving, which adds temporal and state-space complexity.

Limitations of prior work

The introduction of turn constraints (MAPF-T) significantly increases the difficulty of large-scale coordination. The enlarged state space and the temporal dependencies created by rotation actions make it harder to find conflict-free paths, often leading to deadlocks or inefficient, repetitive robot movements in dense environments.

Technical highlights

Implementation of an orientation-aware action model, a recursive priority-inheritance mechanism, cycle handling for circular request chains, a Swap Pattern Detector (SPD), and a k-Push Escape Trigger (PET).

Experiments

Evaluated on MovingAI benchmark maps (empty-32-32, random-32-32-20, room-64-64-8, warehouse-10-20-10-2-2, and den520d) with 5 to 100 agents. A stress test was performed with 1,000 agents. The benchmark compared an earlier turn-aware planner (PIBT-T) and an optimal turn-aware CBS solver. The execution simulation separately used classical PIBT as a baseline.

Results

PIBT-T* achieved high success rates and demonstrated excellent scalability, solving instances with 1,000 agents within seconds. The ablation study confirmed that SPD and PET modules significantly improve success rates and reduce inefficient robot behaviors. Real-robot experiments confirmed the avoidance of deadlocks in physical settings. In the flexible manufacturing simulation, actual makespan fell by 23.3% with ten robots and 20.2% with twenty relative to classical PIBT.

Conclusion

PIBT-T* provides an efficient and scalable solution for MAPF under turn constraints, bridging the gap between discrete path planning and realistic robot motion. It preserves the computational advantages of priority-inheritance methods while ensuring robust local coordination.

Limitations and open problems

The complexity of preprocessing increases with the number of agents and map size, and the current framework relies on a deterministic priority-based decision-making process.

Why it matters to MAPF researchers

This work is crucial for the deployment of large-scale autonomous robot fleets in logistics and manufacturing, where realistic motion constraints must be respected to ensure efficient and safe operation.

Who should read this paper

Researchers working on multi-robot coordination, large-scale path planning, and the application of MAPF in industrial automation.
