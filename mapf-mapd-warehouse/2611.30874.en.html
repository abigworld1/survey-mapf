In short: The paper introduces Lazy-CPBS, a novel algorithm that improves the scalability of continuous-time multi-agent path finding (MAPF) for industrial AGV fleets by delaying node materialization during search.

What is this paper about?

Coordinating fleets of Automated Guided Vehicles (AGVs) in industrial warehouses requires managing complex, highly connected roadmaps while ensuring geometric collision avoidance. Traditional discrete-time MAPF models are insufficient for real-world continuous motion, leading to the use of continuous-time MAPF on roadmaps (MAPF_R). However, in highly redundant industrial layouts, the computational overhead of handling agent interactions and safe-interval updates becomes a significant bottleneck for real-time deployment.

What is new compared with prior work?

The authors analyze the structural properties of the priority-based search space, identifying how priority refinements affect specific agents and when replanning can be avoided. They introduce AGV-MAPF_R-CTC, a configuration-space variant of MAPF_R that allows existing solvers to be reused for AGV fleets with minimal changes. A central contribution is the Lazy-CPBS algorithm, which significantly reduces computational overhead by delaying the materialization of child nodes until they are expanded. Finally, the work provides empirical validation using real-world industrial layouts from a proprietary digital-twin simulator.

What is the key technical idea?

The proposed method builds upon Continuous-Time Priority-Based Search (CPBS), which uses a two-level architecture: a high-level search over priority orderings and a low-level Safe Interval Path Planning (SIPP) solver. The authors identify the boundary conditions required by SIPP under priority-induced constraints to enable earlier solution detection. Lazy-CPBS optimizes this by implementing 'lazy materialization,' where a child's plan is only computed when the node is expanded, thereby avoiding expensive low-level searches for siblings that are never visited. The search is further guided by a replan-list ordering to prioritize more promising branches.

How was it validated?

Validation is conducted through two experimental campaigns: one on standard MAPF_R benchmarks (e.g., den, Berlin, maze, warehouse) and another on industrial AGV roadmaps. The industrial evaluation uses five layouts extracted from a real-world warehouse via a proprietary digital-twin simulator. Performance is measured by the ability to find solutions within a 30-second runtime cap, comparing CPBS, Lazy-CPBS, and an intermediate variant (Eager-RL) across various agent counts and environment complexities.

What are the limitations and open questions?

The analysis reveals that different regions of the priority search space can be explored independently, suggesting potential for parallel high-level exploration. While Lazy-CPBS offers superior robustness and runtime trade-offs, its efficiency gains are bounded by the depth of the solution in the priority tree. The authors note that while the method is effective, future work is needed to provide formal completeness guarantees and to develop multi-threaded, zone-distributed implementations to handle even larger, highly redundant roadmaps.

Detailed section summary

Background

Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF) is a fundamental problem in robotics for coordinating fleets of robots without collisions. While classical MAPF operates on discrete grids and synchronous time steps, real-world applications like AGV logistics require continuous-time planning on geometric roadmaps (MAPF_R) to account for arbitrary motion durations and continuous collision avoidance.

Limitations of prior work

In continuous-time MAPF_R, the complexity of interaction handling increases sharply as congestion emerges. In industrial settings, the high connectivity of roadmaps amplifies the number of potential conflicts, making the search for valid priority orderings computationally expensive due to frequent low-level replanning and safe-interval updates.

Technical highlights

The paper formalizes AGV-MAPF_R-CTC, which generalizes MAPF_R to directed, non-strict roadmaps representing vehicle configurations (pose). It also identifies the specific boundary conditions in SIPP that are necessary to maintain consistency when applying priority-induced reservations.

Experiments

Experiments were performed on an Intel Core i7-12700H CPU with 16 GB RAM. The industrial campaign involved 25 random instances per layout, using random or deterministic restarts to find solutions within a 30-second limit. The study included an ablation study to isolate the effects of lazy materialization and child ordering.

Results

Results demonstrate that Lazy-CPBS substantially reduces the overhead of CPBS, particularly in industrial layouts. It maintains a more consistent robustness-to-runtime trade-off compared to heuristic prioritized planning methods. Furthermore, CPBS-based solvers scale to much higher agent counts than optimal MAPF_R solvers, and Lazy-CPBS is shown to be more efficient than the standard CPBS approach.

Conclusion

The study concludes that priority-based continuous-time MAPF is a viable and robust approach for industrial AGV coordination. Lazy-CPBS, in particular, bridges the gap between theoretical MAPF algorithms and practical industrial requirements by providing a scalable, efficient solution for complex roadmap environments.

Limitations and open problems

The effectiveness of the lazy materialization strategy is limited by the depth of the solution within the priority tree. Additionally, the paper does not establish formal theoretical guarantees for the completeness of the Lazy-CPBS algorithm.

Why it matters to MAPF researchers

This work is important for advancing the practical application of MAPF in industrial automation, providing a scalable framework for managing large-scale AGV fleets in real-time.
