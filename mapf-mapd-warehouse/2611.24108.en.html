In short: The paper proposes FOCBS, an algorithm that solves multi-agent path finding with unassigned agents by combining Conflict-Based Search for assigned agents with a min-cost flow oracle for unassigned agents.

What is this paper about?

In real-world multi-robot systems like automated warehouses, not all robots have active tasks at every moment. These 'unassigned agents' can either act as static obstacles, which blocks narrow passages, or be sent to designated parking locations, which causes unnecessary travel. This research addresses the Multi-Agent Path Finding with Unassigned Agents (MAPF-UA) problem, where idle robots must be routed to avoid obstructing active robots without incurring excessive movement costs. Assigned robots have individual goals and idle robots may stop wherever they can safely wait. The primary objective is the sum of the times at which assigned robots reach their goals and remain there.

What is new compared with prior work?

The authors introduce an asymmetric decomposition framework for MAPF-UA, separating the problem into a high-level search for assigned agents and a low-level flow problem for unassigned agents. They propose the Flow-Oracle Conflict-Based Search (FOCBS) algorithm, which maintains sequential optimality. A key novelty is the 'flow-conflict branching' mechanism, which identifies min-cut blockers when unassigned agents cannot find paths and converts them into constraints for assigned agents, significantly increasing the success rate compared to simply discarding infeasible paths.

What is the key technical idea?

FOCBS operates on a two-level structure. At the high level, it uses Conflict-Based Search (CBS) to resolve conflicts among assigned agents. Once the search finds mutually conflict-free assigned paths, a 'flow oracle' uses a Time-Expanded Network (TEN) to find optimal paths for unassigned agents using min-cost flow. If the oracle fails to find a valid flow, the algorithm performs flow-conflict branching, using the min-cut from the flow network to generate new constraints on the assigned agents' paths, forbidding one blocking reservation per child so the assigned paths can be replanned.

How was it validated?

The method was validated through extensive benchmark experiments and simulations and physical robot experiments. Scalability tests on standard maps (open grid, random obstacles, bottleneck rooms, and warehouse aisles) showed that FOCBS scales well as the number of unassigned agents increases. Ablation studies measured the effects of flow-conflict branching and specific tie-breaking rules. Furthermore, physical-robot experiments demonstrated that FOCBS reduces fleet travel by 40.8% compared to a baseline that relies on parking-based coordination.

What are the limitations and open questions?

On a finite graph with a feasible solution, FOCBS terminates and globally minimizes the sum of assigned robots' last-arrival times. It minimizes idle moves for the particular primary-optimal paths it returns. Another set of equally good assigned paths might let idle robots move less. Termination is not guaranteed for infeasible instances. Rebuilding the time-expanded network and diagnosing failed flows remain costly on bottleneck maps.

Detailed section summary

Background

Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF) is essential for the operation of automated warehouses and manufacturing logistics. While traditional MAPF assumes all agents have specific goals, real-world scenarios often involve idle robots that occupy space but have no immediate destination.

Limitations of prior work

The MAPF-UA problem is NP-hard and presents a trade-off: treating idle robots as obstacles limits reachability, while assigning them to parking spots increases unnecessary travel. This creates a complex coordination problem between active and idle agents.

Technical highlights

The algorithm utilizes a Time-Expanded Network (TEN) with merge-split edge gadgets to prevent simultaneous opposite moves between agents. The use of a single-commodity flow model for unassigned agents is possible because they do not have individual targets, allowing for efficient polynomial-time solving via min-cost flow.

Experiments

Experiments were conducted on four standard MAPF maps with varying agent loads. The researchers compared FOCBS against several baselines, including CBS-UA, PIBT, MACGA, and LaCAM. The study also included ablation studies to isolate the impact of different components. The scaling test fixed the assigned-agent count and increased the idle load. A separate fixed-density test changed the assigned-to-idle ratio. The usual time limit was 60 seconds.

Results

FOCBS demonstrated superior success rates in high-load scenarios compared to CBS-UA. The flow-conflict branching mechanism was shown to be critical for solving complex instances. Physical experiments confirmed significant reductions in total travel distance for both assigned and unassigned robots. The three physical robots completed 40 batches without observed inter-robot collisions. Relative to parking, idle-robot travel decreased by 80.5% and mean batch completion time fell by 14.7%.

Conclusion

FOCBS provides an efficient and scalable solution for MAPF-UA, successfully bridging the gap between high-level combinatorial search and low-level flow optimization. It proves highly effective for real-world multi-robot coordination where unassigned agents are present.

Limitations and open problems

The primary limitations include the computational overhead of recomputing the TEN in dense environments and the focus on sequential rather than global optimality.

Why it matters to MAPF researchers

This work is significant for the deployment of large-scale multi-robot systems in logistics, where managing idle robots is crucial for maintaining high throughput and minimizing energy consumption.
