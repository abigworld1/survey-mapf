Detailed section summary

Background

Multi-agent pathfinding seeks collision-free trajectories for a team of agents, while lifelong MAPF continuously supplies new tasks as agents complete previous ones. Exact methods such as conflict-based search do not scale comfortably to hundreds of agents, so large deployments commonly use fast, suboptimal planners designed and tuned by experts. Related rule-based approaches include priority inheritance, asynchronous variants, windowed planning, neighborhood search, and traffic-guidance graphs. Other work learns neural policies through reinforcement or imitation learning, and language models have also been considered as online planners, although sequentially emitting moves does not scale well beyond small stylized problems. Automated program synthesis has shown promise for discovering algorithms and discrete heuristics, but its applicability to realistic LMAPF with task assignment, spatial planning, and low-latency execution remained an open question.

Limitations of prior work

The abstract model contains agents, a finite directed configuration graph, and a discrete planning horizon. Vertices represent configurations such as position and orientation, while directed edges represent feasible motions with strictly positive traversal durations. Agents may wait at vertices, but edge traversal is non-preemptive; a conflict map specifies which vertices, edges, or physical configurations cannot be occupied simultaneously. Tasks arrive into an available pool through an exogenous stochastic process, and each task contains an ordered sequence of errands with admissible goal configurations and required service durations. A policy assigns available tasks to idle agents and selects waiting or outgoing-edge actions, subject to assignment, kinematic, collision, and real-time constraints, with the primary objective of maximizing expected completed-task throughput.

Technical highlights

PSCS changes the planner and scheduler implementation itself, rather than only tuning costs on a fixed planning architecture. Candidate evaluation is closed-loop, and execution traces plus profiler logs provide evidence for diagnosing congestion and runtime bottlenecks. The conflict representation is general enough to describe classical vertex and swap conflicts as well as footprint conflicts for continuous warehouse layouts. In the warehouse case, the policy combines congestion-aware dispatch with routing behavior that respects departure commitments. Because the resulting policy is ordinary source code rather than a neural controller queried at runtime, it has no neural inference latency and can be subjected to formal verification under suitable assumptions.

Experiments

The LoRR search used five shared-grid instances and optimized cumulative throughput while enforcing collision avoidance, valid assignments, a one-second step budget, and a 32 GB memory limit. The paper reports that PLATM synthesis took 42.3 wall-clock hours and approximately 700 dollars in API usage. The warehouse study evaluated six operational facilities and an unseen synthetic layout; CGAR required 177.8 active development hours and approximately 4,800 dollars. Coding agents performed the implementation, testing, and diagnosis, while the authors selected objectives and constraints and reviewed the final outputs.

Results

On the densest LoRR instance, the discovered policy exceeded the stronger locally reproduced competition entry by 35.0%. Conclusion

The two case studies support the conclusion that coding-agent-driven search over policy implementations can be a competitive way to design complex LMAPF algorithms. The approach combines automated computational exploration with explicit, inspectable source code instead of choosing between manual engineering and opaque learning alone. The warehouse case additionally demonstrates that a discovered policy can have an analytical progress certificate under stated assumptions. Thus, the paper presents PSCS as a promising engineering methodology, while not claiming that the supplied excerpt resolves its reproducibility, cost, or model-dependence questions.

Limitations and open problems

The demonstrated searches were organic and human-directed, so the evidence does not establish performance from a fully autonomous fixed search harness. Search and simulation costs are substantial, and faster evaluation through temporal parallelization, importance sampling, or surrogate simulators is identified as future work. The codebase may accumulate tightly coupled heuristics as agents address successive edge cases, making later auditing and modification difficult. The safety and completion guarantees apply only under the assumptions used in the warehouse analysis, and the excerpt does not specify how robust those assumptions are to all physical deployment conditions. It also remains open whether a static agentic scaffold would overfit to the limitations of a particular coding-agent model release.

Why it matters to MAPF researchers

The work is important because it expands automated MAPF design from parameter optimization to the structure and implementation of the planner and scheduler. It offers a route toward jointly handling assignment, routing, congestion, and low-latency execution while retaining a source artifact that humans can inspect and formally analyze. The reported gains suggest that this direction can matter in both benchmark competition and industrially motivated simulation. Its broader value is methodological: it links agentic software engineering, program synthesis, combinatorial optimization, and verifiable robotic coordination.
