In short: This paper shows that Continuous-time Conflict-Based Search, or CCBS, does not always preserve optimal solutions because its implemented branching rule can remove valid plans from the search. It introduces OC-CBS, a drop-in replacement whose sound branching rule provides formal guarantees of optimality and solution completeness for solvable continuous-time MAPF instances.

What is this paper about?

The paper studies continuous-time Multi-Agent Path Finding with geometric motion, timed actions, waiting, and collision avoidance among multiple agents sharing an environment. In classical MAPF, time is discretized, whereas the MAPF-R formulation considered here represents motion and action durations in continuous time. Each agent must move from a start vertex to a goal vertex, and the central objective is to find collision-free plans with minimum cost, primarily sum of costs, or SOC. The work focuses on the theoretical reliability of exact search rather than merely finding feasible paths.

What is new compared with prior work?

The first contribution is a framework for analyzing CCBS-style branching rules through the notion of sound constraint pairs. A branching rule is sound when its expansion does not remove all valid plans that could be part of a solution. The paper then introduces Optimal Continuous-time Conflict-Based Search, or OC-CBS, with a branching rule satisfying this condition. It proves that OC-CBS is exact and solution complete under the stated assumptions, and argues that it can replace CCBS in existing methods that relied on CCBS correctness guarantees. What is the key technical idea?

CCBS-style algorithms search a constraint tree. When a collision is found in a set of individual agent plans, the algorithm creates child nodes by adding constraints intended to prevent the conflicting behavior. The paper decomposes motion and vertex restrictions into sets of forbidding constraints and defines soundness in terms of preserving at least one relevant valid plan during branching. It proves that the soundness of the branching rule is equivalent to the soundness of the underlying pair of constraint sets. This result is combined with a lower-bound property on constraint-tree objective values supplied by the low-level planner, and with a finite-termination argument, to establish exactness and solution completeness. The OC-CBS implementation builds on the public CCBS implementation and uses analytical interval computation rather than a binary search with a specified precision. The reported implementation assumes circular agents, straight-line traversals, constant speed, and finite-precision arithmetic.

What are the limitations and open questions?

The central failure mode is that CCBS's implemented branching rule is unsound for some move-wait conflicts. It can remove valid solutions from the search, which means CCBS can return a suboptimal solution even though it has commonly been treated as an exact baseline. This matters beyond CCBS itself because theoretical guarantees of methods built on top of it may depend on a correctness property that does not actually hold. OC-CBS addresses the root cause by changing the branching rule rather than abandoning conflict-based search. Detailed section summary

Background

Multi-Agent Path Finding asks several agents to reach individual goals in a shared environment without collisions or deadlocks. It has applications in warehouses, factories, video games, traffic management, airport surface operations, and search and rescue. Classical optimal MAPF is NP-hard, motivating both suboptimal strategies such as prioritization and restricted time windows and exact methods such as Conflict-Based Search. Continuous-time variants are needed when motion durations and collision timing cannot be represented faithfully by discrete time steps.

Limitations of prior work

A MAPF-R instance is represented using a directed graph embedded in a two-dimensional metric space, together with mappings from agents to start and goal vertices and a finite set of move actions. A move action describes one possible way to traverse an edge and has a motion function from time to position, with a duration and specified start and end locations. An individual plan is a finite sequence of timed actions whose transitions are temporally and spatially consistent. The final action is an infinite wait, capturing that an agent remains at its last location indefinitely. The optimization problem is to select valid plans for all agents while avoiding collisions and minimizing the chosen objective.

Technical highlights

The paper distinguishes soundness, exactness, and solution completeness. Soundness concerns whether branching preserves valid solutions; exactness concerns returning an optimal solution; solution completeness concerns terminating with a solution on every solvable instance. A key lemma establishes that a pair of constraint sets is sound exactly when the corresponding branching rule is sound. The main theoretical route is then to combine a sound branching rule with a lower-bound property for constraint-tree nodes and a finite-termination proof. These ingredients support the claims that OC-CBS is exact and solution complete under the paper's assumptions.

Conclusion

The paper concludes that OC-CBS is an exact and solution-complete continuous-time MAPF solver under finite-precision arithmetic and the stated modeling assumptions. It repairs a gap caused by the invalidated correctness guarantees of CCBS and can serve as a replacement in systems that depend on CCBS. The analytical treatment of constraint soundness and termination is also intended as a reusable foundation for evaluating and designing future CCBS-like solvers. The authors characterize the work both as a correction to the CCBS lineage and as a step toward a more rigorous theory of optimal continuous-time MAPF.

Limitations and open problems

Runtime competitiveness is reported, but the supplied excerpt does not establish dominance over CCBS across all instances or hardware. Why it matters to MAPF researchers

The work is important because it challenges an assumption embedded in a widely used exact baseline for continuous-time MAPF. It shows that a theoretically plausible search framework can lose optimality through a concrete implementation choice in its branching rule. OC-CBS supplies both a corrective algorithm and explicit criteria for checking whether future CCBS-like methods preserve solutions. This can improve the reliability of comparative studies and of continuation methods whose guarantees depend on an exact low-level or high-level solver.
